# BARN world catalog

715 worlds carry the three colour swatches and can run the scavenger
task. This file ranks them by how hard they are to navigate, so runs
can be chosen deliberately instead of by habit.

Metrics come from `barn/grids/grid_NNN.npy` at 0.15 m per cell. Every
BARN grid is walled on all four borders; the wall ring is stripped
before measuring, so these numbers describe the interior only.

Regenerate with the snippet at the end of this file.

## What is measured

| metric | meaning |
|---|---|
| **occupancy** | fraction of interior cells that hold an obstacle |
| **min gap** | narrowest passage on the widest route across the world. The traversable corridor is found by binary search on a clearance threshold, keeping the two ends connected; the value is twice the surviving clearance, i.e. the free width the robot must fit through |
| **open space** | fraction of free cells with at least 0.5 m of clearance — how much room there is to manoeuvre rather than thread |
| **score** | 0-1 difficulty: `0.45 x occupancy + 0.35 x (1 - gap) + 0.20 x (1 - open)`, each term normalized across the set. Higher is harder |

Occupancy alone is a poor guide. A world can be sparse yet brutal if
its one route pinches to 0.30 m, and dense yet easy if the clutter sits
in open bays. Gap width and open space carry that difference.

The worlds worth knowing by number.

| **768** | 9.2 % | 0.42 m | 6.3 % | HARD | 527/715 | The main development world. Tight gaps and very little open space. |
| **305** | 17.7 % | 0.30 m | 64.1 % | EXTREME | 715/715 | Hardest in the set. Dense clutter and a 0.30 m pinch. |
| **410** | 8.1 % | 0.42 m | 5.1 % | HARD | 439/715 | Median-difficulty check. |
| **900** | 1.7 % | 0.90 m | 46.5 % | EASY | 97/715 | Nearly empty outlier — a sanity world, not a benchmark. |

Worlds 020 and 768 bracket the useful range: the same unchanged stack
completed the three-colour task in **9.7 min on 020** and **28.9 min on
768**. On 020 both early swatches converted 22-25 s after first
sighting with no re-aiming at all; on 768 the same swatches cost
minutes of orbiting. Approach cost tracks clutter closely.

Search cost does not. The final swatch took roughly 82 % of the run on
both worlds, because finding something not yet visible is a coverage
problem rather than an approach problem.

**Report results across tiers, not from one world.** A single-world
number mostly measures the world.
